# Directions beyond the existing literature (2026-11-09)

Starting point (what our own data says, not what the literature says):
- The system-attributable error is small and stable (0.44% of releases); most remaining error is benchmark gold, time, rank or scope ambiguity, and namesakes: questions whose answer is not unique.
- Errors are correlated across models: a 27B veto caught 2 of 18 added errors (S25); independent retrieval cut the added error from 21% to 6.5% but not below 3% (S23).
- Coverage is limited by evidence and question handling, not by the readers' ability to copy a span.
- Where evidence is closed and the gold exact, the machinery gave no wrong definite answer (S24), but readers fail some reasoning (intervals) and abstain on some namesakes.
"Eliminate" is not provable in the open world (programme document, section 1); the achievable form is "convert": every definite claim is either checked against a stated source and condition, or is not made.

| # | direction | what it is | why it is not already covered | evidence it is worth trying | first experiment (cost) | what would kill it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Question resolution before answering | resolve the question into a fully specified query (entity id, time anchor, ranking criterion), answer that, and state the resolution in the output | the literature studies ambiguity benchmarks and clarification; not resolution as a certified output state with an error budget | 17 of the 29 adjudication-kept Mintaka errors (combination system) are time, rank or scope ambiguity that the entity slot cannot touch | classify the kept errors by resolvable-or-not and score the resolved question against the blind verdicts (CPU, existing data, a day) | most of the errors turn out to be gold or question defects that no resolution fixes |
| 2 | A learned error predictor inside the Learn-then-Test bound | features of the candidate (agreement count, page type, title-match rank, namesake count, question type, time flag) feed a score; the threshold is calibrated to 1% as before | calibrated cascades use model uncertainty; we have the structured features of a deductive gate | our LTT baseline on agreement alone gave coverage 36% at 9 errors; a better score can move that frontier | fit on the SimpleQA design pool (2,500 items), test on FRESH-5 and on the new sealed set (CPU, a day) | the frontier does not move beyond agreement plus gate |
| 3 | Hidden-state probes as the ranking score | linear probes on the 4B's hidden states at the answer span predict "this released answer is correct"; the gate stays the certificate | semantic entropy probes exist; none is paired with a deductive gate and a certified bound | matched-coverage comparison showed agreement is as good as the gate on two of three sets, so a better score is the open lever | extract hidden states on the 2,000 S18 items, train on them, test on FRESH-5 at equal coverage (GPU, a few hours; white-box access exists, weights are local) | probe error rate no better than agreement at equal coverage |
| 4 | Training on synthetic closed-evidence worlds with a verifier reward | generate unlimited worlds with exact gold (namesakes, missing facts, computations); fine-tune the reader with rejection sampling or RL where the reward is gate acceptance plus exact correctness | abstention RL uses answer correctness rewards; ours is a deterministic verifier and noise-free gold at any scale | S24: the machinery is exact on these worlds; readers fail intervals and some namesakes, which training can target | LoRA on 20k generated items; test on a fresh synthetic seed and on the real design sets (GPU, a day); earlier trained-abstention runs in this project (docs/REPORT.md) mostly increased abstention, so this is risky | it improves only the synthetic distribution; real-data error or coverage does not move |
| 5 | Structured grounding (answer = query result) | for relations that live in a knowledge graph, produce a SPARQL query, execute it, answer with the result, abstain on failure | text-to-SPARQL QA exists; a certified silent-error bound for it, evaluated on time-anchored questions, does not | time-anchored questions are exactly where text sources are stale | the new sealed set (below) answered by a query executor as an extra system (CPU plus the public endpoint) | the executor's error is dominated by query-generation mistakes |
| 6 | Active evidence recovery | when readers disagree or the page is missing, turn the gap into a new search or entity lookup and re-verify | iterative RAG exists (S2G-RAG and others); ours would be gated by the same certificate | retrieval misses are the largest abstention class on DEV | one reformulation round for support-2 items on DEV (GPU plus rate-limited web search, a day) | the new evidence is the same pages (independence lost again) |
| 7 | Independence engineering | corroboration across page types (infobox against prose, list against article) rather than the same page twice | cross-model agreement papers; page-type independence is not studied | S23: independence of the source cut added error threefold | count corroboration on the existing S23 and S25 candidates (CPU, hours) | corroboration rate is too low to keep coverage |
| 8 | Machine-checked statement of the gate | state V1 to V5 and the entity slot in a proof assistant over a formal model of pages and spans | proof-carrying certificates for LLM pipelines exist (not read in full) | would turn the L3 text into a theorem | scope the formal model on the closed-evidence world first (days) | the formal model is too far from the code to mean anything |

Recommendation. Do 1, 2 and 7 first: they use data we already have, take days at most, and test whether the coverage-precision frontier can move without new models.
Do 3 next (GPU, a few hours) because it tests the one lever the matched-coverage result points to. Do 4 only after 1 to 3, because the project's earlier training attempts were negative
and the cost is higher. 5 and 6 depend on the new sealed set. 8 is a paper of its own and does not change error rates.
All of these are tested on design data first and certified only on sealed questions with a pre-declared rule.
