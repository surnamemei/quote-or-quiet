## What this means for us
1. The statistical part of L2 (calibrated thresholds, finite-sample bounds on accepted-answer risk) is not new: BalanceRAG and Judge, Retrieve, or Abstain do it for RAG QA.
   Our claim cannot be the bound itself. It can be: a frozen deductive system, certified once on sealed questions with a pre-declared rule, the measured failure of transfer,
   and the separation of system error from benchmark error with a validated adjudication.
2. The verbatim-span idea is not new either (extractive QA, VerbatimRAG). What we add is typed checks against hash-pinned pages, the entity slot, derivation certificates and
   a measured error budget; none of the abstracts reports such a budget.
3. We have not run BalanceRAG-style calibration; our LTT baseline on the agreement score is the nearest stand-in. A fair head-to-head needs their full papers (assumptions,
   datasets) and, ideally, their code.
4. No search turned up a retrieval-grounded abstention study with a certified bound on SimpleQA (searches cover abstracts only).
Next reading: full texts of BalanceRAG and Judge, Retrieve, or Abstain (assumptions, datasets, coverage) and VerbatimRAG's original paper.


## Full-text reading of the two closest papers (2026-11-09; PDFs in results/lit_pdfs, text extracted with pdftotext)
BalanceRAG (arXiv 2605.20084).
- Guarantee: finite-sample, high-probability control of the selection-conditioned error rate (error among accepted answers) of a two-branch router (LLM-only, RAG fallback),
  by sequential graphical testing over a 2-D threshold lattice (family-wise error control, Clopper-Pearson or Hoeffding bounds). Assumes i.i.d. calibration data.
- Data: TriviaQA, SQuAD v2, Natural Questions; eight backbones from Qwen, Llama and Vicuna; DPR and Sentence-BERT retrievers.
- Risk targets 0.10 to 0.16 (some appendix runs 0.10 to 0.12); under strict targets branch-wise baselines are often infeasible ("-" in Table 1).
- Correctness: semantic similarity to the gold with threshold 0.7 by default; entailment and LLM-judge as robustness checks. Uncertainty: semantic entropy by default.
- Validation: 100 random 50/50 calibration-test splits of the same dataset, test error close to the target. No cross-dataset or shifted-distribution test; the limitations
  paragraph says the guarantee needs calibration and deployment data from the same distribution and may fail if the query distribution, corpus or backbone changes.
Judge, Retrieve, or Abstain (arXiv 2608.17994).
- Guarantee: Clopper-Pearson bound on the false discovery rate of accepted verdicts; two thresholds (parametric judge, web-retrieval re-evaluation) with a lemma that the bound
  holds for routing under i.i.d. data; Bonferroni variant for the threshold search.
- Data: TriviaQA, NQ-Open, HotpotQA, PopQA, 2,000 sampled instances each from the standard held-out split; judges Qwen and Llama sizes; ground truth = dataset gold or a reference
  relevance function. Risk targets 0.05 to 0.30. Validation on 100 random 50/50 splits. One drift experiment: the same questions re-queried three months later; FDR stays below target.
- The task is judging a candidate answer, not answering.
What this changes. (1) Their regimes are 5 to 30% error among accepted answers on benchmarks with lenient gold; a 1% bound over all questions (SER) is a stricter regime
that the cascade baselines report as infeasible at tight targets. (2) Both validate on random splits of one benchmark; neither tests transfer across benchmarks, which is where our
certificate failed. (3) Neither audits gold errors; we found 19 of 28 Mintaka errors were gold problems. (4) Neither has a deductive gate or an entity slot.
What is not shown: that our system beats theirs. We have not run their calibration at a 1% target on our data. VerbatimRAG's original paper was not found in the search
(only the ACL-Verbatim application paper), so its mechanism is described only through that paper's abstract.


## Repositories (checked 2026-11-09 by reading pages; nothing cloned or run)
- BalanceRAG (arXiv 2605.20084): no code link in the paper (PDF links and text searched), none on the arXiv, alphaxiv or catalyzex pages, and a GitHub repository search returned 0 matches.
- Judge, Retrieve, or Abstain (arXiv 2608.17994): same, no code link and 0 matches in a GitHub search. Their numbers cannot be reproduced from released code; a comparison needs our own reimplementation from the paper.
- Verbatim RAG (github.com/KRLabsOrg/verbatim-rag, MIT; PyPI verbatim-rag, verbatim-core): extractive RAG that returns source spans with citations. Its README states a "provenance guarantee, not a truth guarantee"
  (retrieval can be incomplete, sources wrong, extractors can pick partial passages): the same boundary as our L3. Reported scores are extractor-level (word-level F1 53.6 for the 150M ModernBERT model against 48.7 for the best
  LLM extractor on a 100-row benchmark), not end-to-end hallucination rates. Evaluation and reproduction are in a separate repository, KRLabsOrg/acl-verbatim. The README cites a 2025 BioNLP shared-task paper for the original method (not opened).
- Cascaded selective evaluation (github.com/jaehunjung1/cascaded-selective-evaluation; supplementary code for "Trust or Escalate: LLM Judges with Provable Guarantees for Human Judgement", Jung et al.; no license stated in the README):
  CascadedClassifier calibrates an abstention rule with a risk alpha and confidence delta from a calibration split (500 samples in the example). BalanceRAG adapts this framework as its UCB-Cascaded baseline, so it is the nearest public
  implementation of the statistical machinery and a reference for our Learn-then-Test baseline. Not run.
Consequence: our LTT baseline (results/runs/s23/conformal_base.py) stays the stand-in for the single-threshold part of these methods; BalanceRAG's two-branch lattice would need an LLM-only branch score that we have not generated.


## Trust or Escalate code read (2026-11-09; cloned shallow into results/lit_repos/trust_or_escalate, 35 MB, read as data, not run)
cascaded_evaluation/cascaded_classifier.py and util.py (249 lines).
- Risk is the selective risk: errors among accepted samples. The upper bound inverts the binomial: the largest r with binom.cdf(k, n, r) >= delta (brentq), i.e. an exact Clopper-Pearson-type bound on the
  accepted-sample error rate. Thresholds come from a 5,000-point grid, keeping only those with at least 30 accepted calibration samples.
- lambda_hat scans from the strictest threshold downward and stops at the first threshold whose bound exceeds alpha: fixed-sequence testing, the same selection logic as our LTT baseline.
- Quirk: if even the strictest threshold has an upper bound above alpha, the code still returns the strictest threshold ("prevents null-value"), so it does not abstain on everything and the stated guarantee is not met in that case.
- Cascade: each model is calibrated on the samples not accepted earlier, with the same delta at every stage (no visible split of delta across stages; whether the paper splits it was not checked).
- README example: 500 calibration samples, alpha 0.15, selective accuracy about 0.87. Judges: GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-3.5 Turbo, Mistral-7B-Instruct; human-preference data.
Consequences. (1) Our LTT baseline matches this logic; the difference is the risk definition: ours bounds errors over all questions (SER), theirs errors among accepted answers.
(2) In their metric our results are: FRESH-5 frozen system selective risk 0.70%, corrected 97.5% upper bound 2.04% (SER bound 0.76%); Mintaka frozen 4.52% (upper 6.65%), adjudicated 1.45% (2.95%);
PopQA 4.24% (7.00%); LTT baseline at t = 6 on FRESH-5 1.63% (3.31%). At a 1% selective-risk target none of these certifies, including the system's; the SER bound is certifiable at 37% coverage because it is
the selective risk times the coverage. The published methods operate at 5 to 30% selective risk. This must be stated whenever the 0.76% figure is compared with theirs.


## Title and novelty check (2026-11-09, found while choosing a title)
- "When Benchmarks Age: Temporal Misalignment through Large Language Model Factuality Evaluation" (Jiang, Chang, McAuley, Xu; arXiv 2510.07238; EACL 2026 Main): tests five factuality benchmarks against eight LLMs with an
  up-to-date retrieval pipeline; the abstract says a considerable portion of benchmark samples is outdated. The general observation that stale gold inflates apparent hallucination is therefore not new; our contribution
  is the quantification inside a certification (blind, decoy-validated adjudication of a certified system's errors, 19 of 28 on Mintaka). Abstract read; full text not read.
- Title family: "Attribute or Abstain: Large Language Models as Long Document Assistants" (arXiv 2407.07799) and "Don't Hallucinate, Abstain" (arXiv 2402.00367) exist; "Cite or Abstain" is not an exact match but sits in a crowded pattern.
  Searches for the exact phrases "Receipts or Silence" and "Quote or Quiet" found no paper with those titles (search results only, not an exhaustive check).
- HalluLens (arXiv 2504.17550) reports incorrect gold answers in TruthfulQA; PARALLAX (arXiv 2605.17028) and HalluHard (arXiv 2602.01031) concern construction artifacts and separating hallucination from stale information. Seen in snippets, not opened.
