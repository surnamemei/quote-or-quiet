# Roadmap: theory, what is achievable, and the granular steps (written 2026-11-05)

Citation status: **[page]** = I fetched the paper's own arXiv/ACL page and read the abstract; **[snippet]** = seen only in search-result summaries (not verified, may be wrong); **[own]** = measured in this project. Nothing here was verified against full texts.

## 1. What can and cannot be achieved (theory)

1. **Hallucination is a classification problem plus an incentive problem.** Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, Zhang, arXiv 2509.04664 [page]: errors arise as binary-classification errors (the model cannot tell false from true statements), and training/benchmarks reward guessing over admitting uncertainty. A lower-bound formula was reported by secondary sources [snippet] and is not confirmed by the abstract.
   - Consequence for us: for arbitrary long-tail facts a closed-book model cannot discriminate right from wrong, so it must abstain or guess. [own] confirms this: Qwen3.8-27B is correct on 4.4% of SimpleQA Verified questions and wrong on 95.6%; its own "unknown" flag is only weakly informative (precision 8.9% vs 4.4% base).
   - Consequence for scoring: grade with abstention-aware metrics (SER, selective risk, yield), never accuracy alone. Already done.
2. **Knowledge has a storage budget.** Allen-Zhu and Li, arXiv 2404.05405 [page]: about 2 bits of knowledge per parameter in their controlled setting, holding at int8; sparsity such as MoE is listed as a factor (the abstract does not give the MoE result; a ~1.3x reduction appeared in a snippet [snippet]).
   - Consequence: parametric facts that were rare in training are not in the weights at all; no verifier can recover them. Only external evidence can.
   - Dense vs MoE (Qwen3.8-27B vs Flash-Next 125B/6B-active) cannot be settled from this paper; the comparison stays confounded (total params, active params, attention, quantization, runtime).
3. **Teaching a model new facts by fine-tuning raises hallucination.** Gekhman et al., arXiv 2405.05904 (EMNLP 2024) [snippet]: examples with facts the model does not know are learned slowly and, once fit, increase hallucination roughly linearly; filtering/early stopping helps. Follow-up arXiv 2511.02626 [snippet].
   - Consequence: abstention training must not inject facts. Build the training set from the model's own known/unknown split and reward "unknown" only on its unknowns.
4. **Same-model verification shares blind spots.** Evidence: ICLR 2025 generation-verification gap (cross-model gap grows with verifier capability) [snippet]; Valmeekam-style self-verification false-positive rates [snippet, second-hand]; a single-author preprint on correlated errors [snippet, treat as hypothesis]. [own]: across SQuAD 2.0, FaithEval and synthetic data a same-model verifier is not better than one cautious prompt.
   - Consequence: any verifier claim needs an independent checker (other family, tool, or retrieved evidence) and a matched-coverage control. Both are in the protocol.
5. **Guarantees need stated assumptions.** Conformal risk control for abstention gives finite-sample bounds only under exchangeability of calibration and test data; SCoRE (2603.24704) states exchangeability and only asserts a shift extension; UniCR (2509.01455) is withdrawn and must not be cited; see docs/LITERATURE_CHECK.md. Phillips et al., arXiv 2603.21172 [page]: entropy alone is not enough for safe selective prediction; combining entropy with a correctness probe improves the risk-coverage trade-off on TriviaQA, BioASQ, MedicalQA across four model families.
   - Consequence: any "risk <= alpha" statement must name its calibration distribution, and we must test it on a shifted set.
6. **Hidden-state probes are cheap detectors, but beware inflated numbers.** Semantic Entropy Probes, Kossen et al., arXiv 2406.15927 [snippet]. arXiv 2606.02628 [page] (single author, three 7-8B models in 4-bit, TruthfulQA/HaluEval/FEVER/synthetic) reports linear-probe AUROC 0.90-1.00 and sampling baselines at most 0.54. AUROC near 1.0 on paired-label benchmarks is a warning sign for dataset artifacts (answer style, pairing), not evidence of a solved problem. Reproduce only with our programmatic gold and a held-out distribution.
7. **Retrieval moves the ceiling but does not remove errors.** GRAB-RAG (arXiv 2608.22228, single author, 3.8-8B models) [page, via docs/LITERATURE_CHECK.md]: abstention on missing evidence is manageable; misleading or conflicting evidence is the main failure; an NLI verifier fails when parametric memory and the wrong passage agree. GRACE (arXiv 2601.04525) trains sufficiency judgement and abstention with RL.
8. **What this adds up to.**
   - "Eliminate" is unattainable as an unconditional claim for open-world parametric facts (items 1-2).
   - Attainable: **L3** for formal/closed-evidence tasks (code execution, verbatim-quote support); **L2** statistical risk control with a named distribution for open-world tasks; **evidence coverage** as the real bottleneck (item 7 and [own]: the gold answer is present in the retrieved passage for only 42% of the questions with live search and 68% even with the dataset's own Wikipedia source pages).
   - So the programme's measurable objective becomes: maximise correct answers at a fixed, stated silent-error budget, where the budget is met by evidence, calibrated abstention, or a tool, and report which.

## 4. Decision rules (written before further results)
- If S2 leaves coverage under 50% on dev: stop tuning retrieval; treat corpus coverage as the limit and report yield under evidence as the headline.
- If S3 finds no signal with a lower bound above base rate: do not claim any closed-book risk control for long-tail facts.
- If S6 does not beat B2: report that training did not help beyond prompting at this scale; do not scale it up.
- Any step that touches the frozen test ids needs a new freeze entry with predictions first.

**Theory pass 2026-11-05 (docs/THEORY.md).** (1) Any gate on a scalar signal is a point on Y*(eps) = pi * ROC(eps/(1-pi)); our measured systems sit on one such curve (binormal prediction within 5 pts of the measured frontier). Reaching 0.1% SER with useful yield needs a signal AUROC of about 0.98 or more, far above anything measured (0.64-0.94), so further investment in confidence signals is deprioritised. (2) Certificates (exact span + deterministic program) make unsupported answers impossible by construction; attributed claims then fail only through reading errors. (3) Verifying 1% needs about 300 labelled accepted answers and 0.1% about 3,000. Priority order after S8: certificate-constrained generation, reading-specific checks and training, then a larger labelled set for an L2 guarantee.
