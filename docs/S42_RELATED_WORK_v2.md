## 2. What is already done (with evidence)

1. A machine-checked verbatim quote as a condition for showing an answer, with abstention, exists since 2022. GopherCite constrains or parses quotes so they are verbatim and declines below a reward-model threshold (full text read). CAMS drops claims whose quote the matcher cannot locate and drops sentences that fail its verifier (full text read). Zhang et al. check verbatim compliance by script. So item (1) as an idea is not new; only the typed-span, hash-pinned deductive gate and its zero-exception record are the draft's own.
2. Measuring whether the quote shown with an answer is enough to verify it is established. GopherCite raters answer "supported" for exactly this. Zhang et al. run a quote-only judge with human validation and report 37.1% strict support for a model with 98.0% verbatim quotes. CAMS has a three-annotator human study. Liu et al. report citation precision of 74.5%. AIS, ALCE, AttributionBench, VERICITE and eTracer measure sentence or citation support. So item (2) is a known measurement. The draft's version differs in the setting (short-form factoid QA through a deductive gate), in the target (right answers whose quote does not state them, 72 of 75), and in the reporting (blind two-reviewer, decoys, upper bounds).
3. Selecting among several candidate evidence items and abstaining is established, with learned scorers. GopherCite reranks N sampled (answer, quote) pairs with a reward model and declines below a threshold. Zhang et al. search all sections the model read for a replacement span (large judge, verbatim check only). Perez et al. learn sentence selection. CAMS, GRACE and Attribute-First select evidence with trained components.
4. Sufficiency as a release or abstention signal, set-level or passage-level, is established (Joren et al. 2025, SURE-RAG, GRACE, Sato et al. 2026, Jain and Garimella 2026). Counterfactual swap controls exist as test designs (SURE-RAG, Lee et al., Faithfulness-QA, EviScope).
5. Small trained or distilled consistency checkers exist (AlignScore 355M, MiniCheck 770M, HHEM 110M, fine-tuned smaller models in the AttrScore paper, Med-V1 3B). A 0.6B classifier is therefore not a new kind of object.
6. Entity ambiguity in claims about named subjects is a known evaluation problem (D-FActScore, AmbigDocs). The first-match / wrong-occurrence failure is documented (Min 2019, Asai 2022, CAMS error E3).

## 3. What is not found

All statements are limited by the queries listed in section 4; the search tool is US-only and returns summaries.

1. No work found that, together: lists every span a mechanical gate accepts; picks a witness by a fixed checklist without training (frame check, subject link, quote-only reader with swapped-answer control); withholds the answer and quotes nothing when no span passes; and tests the rule on fresh items by blind review with an upper bound near 1% (0.20%, bound 0.71%, 1,017 witnesses). The nearest candidates are GopherCite (learned reward model; human raters; no bound) and the recovery step of Zhang et al. (large judge; analysis; no withholding; no audit of the recovered span in the text I read).
2. No work found that reports, for answers that are already right, the share whose displayed deductive-gate quote does not state the answer (12.5%, 75 of 600; 72 of 75 with right answers).
3. No work found that gives a quote per reading for same-named films or people (Section 5.13), a biography written from the subject's own page and then kept claim by claim by a quote-only judge (5.12), or a two-sentence quote chain with a bridge check (5.16). Related setups exist (D-FActScore, FActScore, AmbigDocs, multi-hop sufficiency papers) but none was found doing these with quotes.
4. No work found training a 0.6B judge on two-reviewer blind labels of "the quote states this answer" and testing transfer to a held-out natural-question set (5.17). I did not run or find a comparison with MiniCheck, AlignScore or HHEM on these labels; the manuscript has no such baseline.
5. No pre-registered blind test of a quote-based release rule in the attribution literature was found. The searches for pre-registration returned nothing on this topic. Absence of the word in abstracts does not show absence in full text.

## 5. Entries in S42_RELATED_WORK.md that are wrong, imprecise or still unverified

1. GopherCite row (section 2): "support is rated by people, not gated" is wrong. A reward model trained on human supported/plausible labels reranks candidates and sets the decline threshold (full text, Sections 2.5, 2.9). The row also omits that the quote is verbatim by constrained sampling or parsing.
2. CAMS row: "No rate of unsupporting quotes; no choice among several quotes" is wrong. It reports a human study (255 claims per system, 89% fully supported, kappa 0.68), has a trained selector and a verifier that drops failing sentences, and names the wrong-occurrence failure (E3).
3. Section 3 intro and SURE-RAG row: "passage level" should be evidence-set level (claim-passage verifier aggregated over the set). The statement "report risk, not a bound near 1%" holds for SURE-RAG (risk 0.1642 at 30% coverage) and Joren; not checked for GRACE or others.
4. DocAttriBench arXiv:2609.20574 is a document-visual-QA grounding benchmark (cs.CV, BMVC 2026), not "evidence-only sufficiency and evidence-masked necessity tests". Remove it from the sufficiency row. arXiv:2512.06476 is Jain and Garimella, Knowing What's Missing (EACL Findings 2026). arXiv:2609.01687 is Sato et al. Both fit the row loosely; the row's description of "evidence-masked necessity tests" is not supported by any page I opened.

## 7. Verdict on the novelty statement

The sentence "We did not find a training-free rule that chooses among all the spans a deductive gate accepts, withholds the answer when none passes, and is tested by blind review of fresh items against a bound near 1%" is consistent with what I found, provided it stays this narrow. It is not safe to say or imply that (a) showing a verified quote with an abstention option, (b) auditing whether the shown quote supports the answer, or (c) choosing among candidate quotes is new. Each is documented, (a) and (c) most completely by GopherCite, (b) by GopherCite, Zhang et al., Liu et al. and CAMS. The defensible novelty is the combination in a short-form factoid setting: a mechanical gate, a measured 12.5% rate of right answers shown with a non-stating quote, a training-free witness rule with a swapped-answer control and withholding, and a pre-registered blind test with an upper bound. The extensions (4) have no direct precedent found, but they rest on design data and the small judge needs a comparison with existing small checkers.
